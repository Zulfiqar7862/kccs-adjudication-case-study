# Where we land

## Decision

Proceed with a bounded, human-controlled evidence workflow and a gated, single-site EU pilot. Do not authorize automated dispositions, cross-tenant precedent retrieval, or a general-purpose model in the initial release. The product should make a human decision supportable and reconstructable; it must not turn incomplete indexes into a recommendation or a favorable assumption.

The proposed first pilot is referenced only as **the EU pilot tenant** (`12_Tenant_Constraints`, row 2). Its residency position, signatory roles, auditability needs, source ownership, and permitted processing define a narrow pilot boundary—not permission to onboard other tenants or reuse their data. The brief’s four deliverables are addressed below: **A. Where you land, and why; B. The design; C. Something running; D. Your working log.**

## A. Where you land, and why

The operational problem is evidence assembly and accountable timing, not a lack of automated verdicts. The reference pack reports 11,400 monthly alerts and 2,050 human-adjudicated cases; these are source-pack aggregates, not independently validated forecasts. The dataset is stratified and cannot establish population rates or prove a pilot outcome. Historical recommendations, dispositions, and notification records are not correctness labels for new cases.

The system should preserve source/version lineage, timestamps, reviewer rationale, and audit events; show ambiguity and missing sources; keep the notification clock separate from disposition; and retain a named human as the final decision authority. Require an explicit go/no-go before the prototype is used as an operational review workspace.

## Assessment of the board targets

The brief (§8A) invites arguing that part of what the board asked for is the wrong thing to want. This section does that for each commitment in brief §6, using pack numbers from `13_Volume_And_Cost` (population) rather than the stratified `01` tab. Capacity figures are back-of-envelope from medians and are assumptions to be replaced by measured means.

### 1. Median alert-to-disposition under 24 hours

**Two clocks.** Disposition latency (median 6.2 days, `13` row 14) is not notification latency: the regulatory window runs from alert receipt and is independent of the signature (`13` row 22). A case can meet one and miss the other, and the pack contains cases notified inside the window and adjudicated weeks later. Report them separately; never let a fast disposition mask a late filing.

**A median can hide a dangerous tail.** Take 1,000 cases: 600 close in 2 hours and 400 take 9 days. The median is 2 hours, the mean about 3.7 days, and 40% of cases wait 9 days. A median under 24 hours is therefore satisfied by closing the easy majority quickly. Proposed metrics: P50, P95 and P99 of disposition latency by tenant, the count of open cases older than a stated age, and the count of cases past their notification window. The median stays as a reported number, not the gate.

**Where the time goes.** Population medians: 96 active minutes and 7 systems per adjudicated case (`13` rows 11, 13), against 6.2 days elapsed. Active time is about 1.1% of elapsed time; the rest is waiting. A capacity check from `13`: 2,050 cases × 96 min ≈ 3,280 h/month, plus about 2,060 non-actionable alerts that reviewers open anyway (22% of the 82%, `13` row 12) × 17 min ≈ 580 h, against 23 reviewers × 1,800 h ÷ 12 = 3,450 h/month. Demand is about 112% of capacity before leave, interruptions or the storm months. The queue is therefore structural, and the lever is minutes per case. If evidence assembly removed 36 of the 96 minutes (an assumption, not a measurement), it would release about 1,230 h/month. Closing a further 690 benign alerts a month (the gap to 70%, below) would release about 195 h. Evidence assembly is the larger lever, and `10_Reviewer_Questions` shows why: the questions needed on every case (SOP revision, stability limit, signatory, reportability, precedent: rows 2, 3, 10, 11, 12) are lookups with typical times of 14, 22, 5, 8 and 20 minutes (69 minutes in all).

### 2. At least 70% of alerts closed without reaching a person

**Denominators.** 78% of the non-actionable alerts are auto-closed and 82% of all alerts are non-actionable, so 0.78 × 0.82 ≈ 64% of all alerts (≈7,290 of 11,400 a month). The 70% target (7,980 alerts) is ≈690 more closures a month, which means closing 70 ÷ 82 ≈ 85% of non-actionable alerts, up from 78%. Two conditions follow, and the second matters more:

1. The closing logic may miss at most about 15% of benign alerts (they then reach a person).
2. Zero genuine excursions may be auto-closed. With about 2,050 genuine cases a month, a 1% false-close rate is about 20 genuine excursions a month closed without a human, some of them reportable (28 of the 420 sampled events are).

**Definition.** "Closed without reaching a person" must mean a non-actionable closure with a recorded closure type (duplicate, within permitted allowance, equipment event with no product loaded, sensor artefact, dock exposure) and its evidence preconditions, reversible and retained like a disposition. It must never mean a product disposition (brief §6, the quality sponsor's condition). A closed alert that is reportable still carries its notification obligation.

**The baseline is unaudited.** README row 36: nobody has audited the auto-closed population, so the 78% baseline carries an unknown false-close rate. Before the target is raised, run blind human sampling of auto-closures by closure type; a quota optimised without that guard rewards closing, not correctness. Prototype status: it closes nothing; all 420 events are `ABSTAIN` (186 incomplete evidence, 234 blocked by conflicts).

**The incentive.** The business objective is sound: fewer benign alerts reaching reviewers is real saving, and the existing rules engine keeps running and keeps improving. But the quota targets the cheaper lever. The reviewer's day, per `10` and `13`, is spent looking things up (median 96 active minutes, 7 systems), so evidence assembly, not decision automation, is where the 24-hour target is won (section 1). Recommended framing: a ceiling on the false-close rate, with 70% as a secondary figure.

### 3. Full disposition basis within an hour, retained seven years

Unchanged from [risks](09-risks-assumptions-and-open-questions.md): one hour is achievable only as retrieval of a package frozen at signature, not as on-demand assembly. The inspector's four items (SOP revision in force, stability data, temperature series, approver; brief §3) define the minimum package. The pack holds only index metadata for the SOP and stability items, no temperature series and no signer identity, which is why capture at signature (R-11, R-15) matters. Retention start, legal hold and WORM storage remain open.

### 4. Inside the tab 13 cost ceiling

Model and retrieval allowances sum to USD 2,939 against USD 3,000 (R-13, A-04). Because the first release has no model in the disposition path (proposed), tokens are not the binding cost; fixed infrastructure is, and `13` row 27 excludes ingestion, storage, indexing and seven-year retention from the ceiling. One always-on self-hosted instance (USD 4,082.40/month) already exceeds it. The CFO's "quiet Sunday" test is therefore a fixed-cost test: the design must be priced at zero incidents and zero reviews.

### 5. No notification outside its window

Software cannot guarantee zero late notifications. Filing is a human or portal action outside the platform, regulator-side receipt is outside its view, and the inputs (reportability, windows, calendars, timezones) are human-approved policy. In the pack, 14 of 28 reportable sampled events were notified late, all in the 72 h and 24 h markets, with notification up to 217 h after alert (a stratum built to include them, not a rate). What software can guarantee, and what the design is built to prove:

- **Exists:** an obligation is created from alert receipt for every alert, including auto-closed ones, before any queue decision.
- **Visible:** open obligations are listed by due time, per tenant, independent of case state.
- **Escalated:** escalation ladders run on obligation age, not queue position; reserved capacity for obligations during a storm (R-14).
- **Loud on failure:** an unknown window is an urgent review (never "not required"); a stalled clock worker, outbox failure or breach raises an alert; a missing policy fails closed.

The metric is the count of obligations that were missing, silent or unescalated (target zero, a software invariant) and the count past window (an outcome, governed with manual fallback). The prototype creates obligations and local reminders; it does not file, and a manually recorded receipt is human-attested.

### 6. First customer in 90 days; all 34 in nine months

Day 90 is a go/no-go for one tenant (see [roadmap](10-future-roadmap.md)). Nine months for 34 is a sequence, not a commitment: 17 of the 34 tenants are bound by a residency clause (3 named plus 14 of the 26 unlisted), 7 prohibit third-party model APIs (T-VIRENT plus 6 of the 26 unlisted), and constraints for the 26 unlisted tenants are known only as counts (`12` row 10, A-14). I recommend the board commit to the gated order and report slips with the failed gate.


## B. The design

1. **Ingest one authorized snapshot at a time.** Keep workbook digest, source sheet/row, and import observation time. Exact replay is idempotent; a different snapshot is rejected rather than silently merged. Reconciliation and multi-source refresh need an explicit design.
2. **Analyze for evidence readiness, not disposition.** Resolve candidates within tenant and time context. Missing or ambiguous required evidence blocks review. Keep source indexes non-authoritative; reviewer attestations must refer to recorded evidence.
3. **Keep clocks and decisions separate.** Create the reporting obligation from alert data and an approved tenant/jurisdiction policy. Do not infer a universal deadline, reset the clock on case reopen, or equate a reminder attempt with successful external notification. Human-attested receipt remains unverified until an appropriate integration and evidence process exist.
4. **Make workflow changes attributable and concurrency-safe.** Require expected case versions, idempotency keys, role checks, and an append-only event history. Preserve prior revisions on reopen. Hash-chain verification detects some alteration but does not provide WORM retention, legal non-repudiation, or factual validation.
5. **Treat the current implementation as local-only.** The repository currently provides a Python/SQLite service, a loopback HTTP adapter, CLI, workbook reader, and synthetic demo. Static bearer credentials, SQLite durability, notification processing, and policy parsing are prototype boundaries. Production identity, TLS, managed durable storage, verified integrations, recovery procedures, and validated retention remain separate work.

## Deliberate non-goals

The first release does not make disposition decisions, retrieve cross-tenant precedent, or use a general-purpose model to decide cases. It does not guess identities, synthesize missing measurements, infer universal deadlines, or treat an index match as proof of a source document's validity. These exclusions preserve human authority and prevent incomplete or ambiguous evidence from appearing conclusive; related assumptions and board counterarguments are in [risks and open questions](09-risks-assumptions-and-open-questions.md).

The prototype's actual feature boundary and production gaps are summarized in the [executive summary](00-executive-summary.md).

## Critical working component and current implementation

**Chosen critical component: the transactional evidence/lifecycle/clock kernel.** Its hardest invariant is that incomplete or changed evidence must never silently become an approved disposition, while an independent regulatory deadline continues through waits, retries and reopening. A dashboard would not establish that invariant; durable transactions, independent actors, packet versions and failure-path tests do. External evidence and filing dependencies are explicitly stubbed rather than represented as successful integrations.

The current source implements workbook snapshot import, tenant-scoped candidate analysis and blockers, a SQLite case/revision workflow, evidence review, independent signatures, optimistic versioning, idempotency, hash-chained audit export/verification, notification obligations, and an internal reminder outbox. A manually recorded receipt is explicitly human-attested, not delivery-confirmed. `tick` is an internal stub and does not file with or notify a regulator. The evidence analyzer reports candidates and blockers; it does not adjudicate.

The current code does **not** implement production identity or TLS, external document/source retrieval, an external notification or filing integration, independently verified receipt ingestion, multi-snapshot reconciliation, WORM retention, or automated decision recommendations. Public examples and tests must remain synthetic. Private workbook checks are opt-in through an explicit `SENTINEL_WORKBOOK` path and must not emit or commit raw data or reports.
